# LIBERO-90-C: the split, the audit, our checkpoint (plan v4, P0 and P1)

Plan: docs/PLAN_V4.md 3.1 and 8 (P0, P1). Live state: results/l90c/STATUS.md.

## 1. The split (P0; experiments/c_split.py, ledger c_split)

**Atoms** are read by rule from each task's BDDL (lifelong_ood/policy/factors.py). A task is a list of steps, one per goal atom,
so LIBERO-90's "X and Y" tasks and LIBERO-10's two-step tasks are decomposed into their steps. Each step gives a verb (the BDDL
predicate), an object (the moved object, or the articulated fixture) and a target (receptacle plus region; the drawer for Open
and Close). The task adds its scene and its instance qualifiers. **Compounds** per step: object x target, verb x object,
object x scene, target x scene.

**Divergences** follow Keysers et al. (1912.09713): D_A = 1 - C_0.5 over atom frequencies, D_C = 1 - C_0.1 over compound
frequencies, with C_a(P||Q) = sum p^a q^(1-a), P = training, Q = test.

**Part set** (held out of every training task, one per role, the plan's own examples): ketchup (object), desk_caddy
(target), Turnon (verb). It takes 21 LIBERO-90 tasks. LIBERO-90 scenes share props, so ketchup still sits in 17 training
tasks as a distractor; it is never named or moved there.

**Search.** Seeded simulated annealing (8 restarts) over the combination test set W, from the 68 remaining LIBERO-90 tasks
(task 51 has no episode in the IPEC LeRobot port). Hard constraint: every atom of every test task is in at least 2 training
tasks. Grading: a test task's bin is set by the fraction u of its compounds absent from training (u = 0, 0 < u < 0.4,
u >= 0.4). The objective asks for at least 8 tasks per bin, bin CDs near 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5, and the smallest D_A.

**Suite additions.** A LIBERO-Spatial, -Object, -Goal or -10 task joins training only if none of its compounds is held out
and none of its atoms is a part atom. The LIBERO-10 tasks that fail this are listed as `two_step_test`, the productivity
tests.

**Sealed third.** One third of each bin and of the part set (stratified by role), chosen by a seeded hash, goes to
results/l90c/split_sealed.json. That file is gitignored. split.json keeps salted hashes, and lifelong_ood/l90c.py refuses
a sealed task unless it gets an m3_prereg run id on bench libero_90_c and L90C_UNSEAL is set to that same id.

### Result (frozen 2026-10-22, seed 20261022)

| set | tasks | notes |
|---|---|---|
| training | 44 LIBERO-90 cells + 32 suite tasks = 76 | suite: all 10 Spatial, Object minus ketchup, Goal minus 3 and 7, LIBERO-10 1/4/6/7/9 |
| test, CD bin "0.1" (u = 0) | 8 (bin D_C 0.139) | e.g. 22 "close the bottom drawer of the cabinet" (KITCHEN_SCENE4; 23 trains) |
| test, CD bin "0.3" (0 < u < 0.4) | 8 (bin D_C 0.379) | e.g. 9 "black bowl on the plate", KITCHEN_SCENE1 |
| test, CD bin "0.5" (u >= 0.4) | 8 (bin D_C 0.588) | e.g. 36 "white bowl on the plate" (KITCHEN_SCENE7) |
| part | 21 (3 ketchup, 13 caddy, 5 Turnon) | part D_C 0.738, D_A 0.501 |
| sealed (of the above) | 3 per bin + 7 part | examples above are dev tasks; logs and docs never name a sealed task |

**Atom divergence: the 0.02 target is not feasible here.** The split reaches D_A = 0.137 between the LIBERO-90 training cells
and the 24 test tasks, and 0.189 against all training (the suite additions are mostly bowls and plates). D_A is a sampling
measure. Random 24-task test sets drawn from the same pool give 0.180 on average (5th percentile 0.132, minimum of 500 draws
0.102), and a direct D_A-only swap search stalled at 0.117. With about 130 test atom tokens over about 70 atom types, the
noise floor of 1 - Bhattacharyya is already about 0.1 (roughly (K-1)/8 (1/n_test + 1/n_train)). Object, target and scene atoms
each sit near 0.22-0.28 on their own; verbs sit at 0.016. The constraint that carries the intent, every test atom in at least
2 training tasks, holds exactly (the minimum count is 2). D_A is minimised and reported beside its null distribution
(split.json checks.atom_divergence_null).

**Bins.** All three bins reach 8 tasks. More would cost training cells: 68 tasks must cover training, three bins and their
sealed thirds. Per-task CDs (single-task D_C) run higher than the bin CDs: 0.26 / 0.46 / 0.66 on average.

## 2. The contamination audit (P0; experiments/c_audit.py, ledger c_audit)

Following the I6 method (frozen flow-matching loss with fixed (t, noise) draws per frame, shared by every model), we score
pi05_base (openpi's base, under the LIBERO transforms and pi05_libero's norm stats; config pi05_base_libero) and pi05_libero
on 2 demos x 16 frames of every LIBERO-90 training cell, every DEV test and part task, and pi05_libero's 40 fine-tuning tasks.
The sealed tasks are never read. The readout:

- pi05_base's test-minus-train and part-minus-train gaps (expected near 0 whether it saw everything or nothing);
- the ratio of pi05_base's LIBERO-90 loss to pi05_libero's loss on its own seen data (l40): near 1 would mean pre-exposure;
- the ratio of pi05_base's LIBERO-90 loss to pi05_base's loss on l40. LIBERO-40 and LIBERO-90 come from the same benchmark,
  so a base that saw one and not the other would show a gap.

Numbers: section 5.

## 3. pi05_l90c (P1; experiments/c_data.py, c_train_pi05.py, lifelong_ood/policy/l90c_config.py)

- **Data.** LeRobot dataset `lol/libero_l90c` (HF_LEROBOT_HOME). It holds the LIBERO-90 training cells from
  IPEC-COMMUNITY/libero_90_no_noops_lerobot (each task's own scene's episodes; every episode the port has, 28-50 per task
  rather than 50, because the no-noop replay dropped some), and the suite additions from physical-intelligence/libero
  (pi05_libero's own data). Both use pi05_libero's conventions: gripper -1 open / +1 close, 8-D state, 256 px frames, fps 10 as
  physical-intelligence/libero declares it. The converter refuses any task outside split.json's training list. Norm stats are
  computed on this dataset only.
- **Recipe.** pi05_libero's TrainConfig (pi0.5, action horizon 10, continuous state dropped, LIBERO transforms, AdamW with clip
  1.0, EMA 0.999, constant lr 5e-5 after warmup) from `pi05_base`, full fine-tune in openpi's JAX trainer with FSDP over 4 A40s.
  Two deviations: the batch is chosen by the timing job (pi05_libero used 256, which is out of budget), and warmup is 1k steps
  rather than 10k.
- **Timing** (4 x A40, FSDP 4, results/l90c/train_log.jsonl). Batch 32: 11.41 s/step, 2.8 samples/s. Batch 64: 13.34 s/step,
  4.8 samples/s, 1.7x the throughput. Peak 27 GB per GPU at either batch. The main run uses batch 64: 14.9 A40-h per 1k steps,
  about 1,000 steps per 4-h job after 3-7 min of startup, checkpoints every 500 steps (42 GB, saved async in about 70 s, kept
  every 5,000). Parity is read at the kept checkpoints (5k, 10k, 15k, ...), and the run stops at the first that reaches parity
  (the learning rate is constant, so stopping early changes nothing else). pi05_libero's own budget was 30k x 256 = 7.7M
  samples; 10k x 64 is 0.64M samples (1.3 epochs of our 495k frames). The weights are converted to PyTorch (openpi's converter) for PolicyBackbone("pi05_l90c"); backbone.py
  registers the config at runtime.
- **Parity** (experiments/c_parity.py, ledger c_parity). Per-task success over 20 episodes (init states 0-19), MODE=ours,
  through roll_pack.sbatch. pi05_l90c on its 76 training tasks is compared with pi05_libero on its 40.

### 3.1 pi05_l90c_fac: factored conditioning (prepared, NOT launched; waits on the pp pre-checks of pi05_l90c)

The protos dev pre-check on pi05_libero (results/protos/pp2_dev.json, pp3_dev.json) found part-prompt modes near the null
mode (0.036 rms), with attribution at the null level. The VLA ignores part prompts. `pi05_l90c_fac`
(lifelong_ood/policy/l90c_config.py) trains on the same data, steps, batch and norm stats as pi05_l90c and changes one thing.
Per sample, the instruction is replaced
- with probability 0.3 by one of its part prompts, chosen uniformly: object-only ("the white mug"), goal-only ("on the
  plate") or verb-only ("put"). These are the plain variant of lifelong_ood/protos/store.py's rule (`parts_of`), tabled in
  results/l90c/fac_prompts.json for the 73 training instructions;
- with probability 0.1 by null ("").

The action target is unchanged. This is the per-factor dropout of Factored Diffusion Policies (2605.22596), which gives eps of
about uncond + the sum of the factor deltas, and CRAFT's counterfactual alignment (2610.00524). It gives part prompts a
trained meaning, while the relation R is still extracted once, before any adaptation. It runs as an openpi data transform
(`FactoredPrompt`) at the head of the LIBERO data transforms, so inference is untouched. Its RNG is per loader process.
On 10,000 draws for "put the white mug on the plate" it gave 60.3 / 10.2 / 9.7 / 9.7 / 10.2 % instr / null / object / goal /
verb. Launch: the same command as pi05_l90c with `pi05_l90c_fac` (c_train_pi05.py takes --fac, section 5).

**Cost** (from the timing jobs): 14.9 A40-h per 1k steps at batch 64 on 4 A40s. The same step count as pi05_l90c's parity
step costs 149 A40-h at 10k steps and 223 A40-h at 15k.

## 4. Planned, not built: the CRAFT-style 4x4 cell (candidate pool ii)

**Scene.** One tabletop scene, authored as BDDL with LIBERO's generation utilities (libero/libero/utils/bddl_generation_utils,
problem class LIBERO_Tabletop_Manipulation, the main_table of LIBERO-Spatial). It holds four cubes (red, green, blue, yellow)
and four plates of the same four colours. Each cell is "put the <c1> cube on the <c2> plate", goal (On cube_c1 plate_c2):
16 cells sharing one layout distribution.
- Objects. LIBERO has no coloured cube or coloured plate. Add two object classes in our own module, registered through LIBERO's
  `register_object`: a `ColoredCube` (robosuite BoxObject, about 4.5 cm, rgba per colour, friction as the LIBERO-Object boxes)
  and a `ColoredPlate` (LIBERO's plate mesh with its material rgba overridden). Each is registered under four names
  (red_cube ... yellow_plate), so the BDDL's types carry the colour and factors.py parses them unchanged.
- Placement. Cubes and plates are sampled in disjoint regions (cubes front half, plates back half), with the colour-to-slot
  assignment shuffled per init state so that position never encodes colour. Fifty fixed init states per cell, LIBERO's
  `init_states` format.
- Split. Train on the 4 same-colour cells (the diagonal) and test the 12 off-diagonal ones. Every cube colour and every plate
  colour is then seen as an atom, and every off-diagonal compound is new. In the MCD terms of section 1 this gives atoms
  cube colour / plate colour, compound cube x plate, and u = 1/2 for every test cell.
- Caveat to check first. VLAs under-read colour words (LIBERO-Plus, CRAFT). The diagonal-only training makes "colour of the
  cube = colour of the plate" a shortcut. The plan wants that confound: it is the test of whether the policy binds the two
  words separately.

**Demos** (labelled as generated in every row that uses them):
1. Source demo per cell. A scripted privileged-state pick-and-place: approach above the cube, descend, close, lift, move
   above the plate, descend, open. It is driven by the OSC controller LIBERO uses, at 20 Hz, from sim poses. This is enough
   because a cube has one canonical top grasp. Each run is checked by the BDDL goal predicate.
2. Fifty demos per cell from either generator:
   - the repo's v2 generator (experiments/p38_generate.py --rules v2): retarget the one source demo to each init state in the
     object's frame (approach relative to the cube, carry relative to the plate), keeping successes only. Already used in
     ledger g38_generate tag demo_retarget_v2 (386 successes);
   - MimicGen (2310.17596): annotate the source demo's two subtasks (grasp the cube, place on the plate) and let MimicGen
     transform and stitch segments. It needs robosuite 1.4 + MimicGen installed in the LIBERO env.
   The v2 generator is the default: it exists, runs in our LIBERO env and is validated. MimicGen is the published reference to
   cite, or to run if a reviewer asks.
3. Conversion. Record images and states with libero_client's recording format, convert to the LeRobot layout of section 3,
   and filter to the 256 px no-noop convention (drop zero-action frames, as the no-noop ports did).
- Cost. About 16 cells x 50 demos x about 10 s of sim = about 2.5 CPU-h, plus rendering on an EGL GPU node (the v2 generator
  runs on a GPU node with --workers).
- Not in pi05_l90c v1. The 4 training cells would have to join pi05_l90c's training set (a retrain or a short continued
  fine-tune from it). That decision belongs to the user once the cell exists.

**Audit** (ledger c_audit, 2026-10-22). Mean flow loss over real action dims; 44 training cells, 15 dev test, 14 dev part,
40 l40 tasks; 2 demos x 16 frames x 4 fixed draws per task.

| model | LIBERO-90 train | dev test | dev part | l40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pi05_base | 0.353 | 0.353 | 0.339 | 0.377 |
| pi05_libero | 0.356 | 0.352 | 0.286 | 0.014 (its fine-tune data) |

pi05_base shows no differential exposure: test minus train is +0.0001 and the AUC of train < held-out is 0.43. On LIBERO-90
its loss sits 25x above a seen level (pi05_libero on l40), and on LIBERO-40 it sits at the same level (0.377). So it looks
unexposed to either. The test has one limit: a uniform, low-weight exposure to all of LIBERO in pretraining would raise every
column alike, and this design cannot tell that apart from no exposure. It can only say that such exposure is far from what
fine-tuning gives.
