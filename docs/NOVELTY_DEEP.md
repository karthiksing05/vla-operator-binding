# Five preprints crowd the binding theory's flanks

As of 26 October 2026, the theory as a whole is new, but the qualitative story behind it is not. "VLAs keep their motor skill and get the referent wrong" has been reported repeatedly. Three preprints from September and October 2026 tell most of the surrounding story: **ReGuide** factors success into a binding term and a competence term and fixes the binding externally. **"When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories"** (arXiv 2609.39971) finds that pi0.5 retrieves a trained "trajectory family" while visually tracking a displaced object, and moves it with a prefix-KV swap. **CRAFT** trains pi0.5 only on demonstrated combinations and calls the scene "a proxy for the instruction." Four things remain unclaimed in every source checked. The first is the action-level mixture π(a|o,ℓ) = Σ_b P(b|o,ℓ) O(a|o,p_b), with an exemplar P and a pose-equivariant O. The second is the localization of binding to a scene-wide code in deep image tokens, with the region splice inert (C1). The third is the object-frame versus world-frame operator measurement shared across 19 types (C2). The fourth is the coverage-by-phase commitment result (C3), together with predictions (a) and (b) and the relational sub-region failure class (C5). C4, the claim that internal handles fail, is new as a systematic result, but it has to concede that CAG already failed in ReGuide and has to account for CRAFT's partial success with CAG-VA. The testbed is a differentiator against ReGuide and every interpretability paper, all of which use public suite-trained checkpoints. It is not a differentiator against CRAFT. So the paper should claim the mechanism, the measurements and the graded predictions, and should not claim to have discovered the WHERE/HOW split. A solution can only be novel if it acts on P, inside the policy's scene code or through O's equivariance, before the grasp, and without the external pose override that defines ReGuide.

One caveat applies throughout. Most sources were checked through their arXiv abstract pages or partial HTML reads, using a fetch tool that summarizes pages. The full texts of TAG, IGAR, CAG, Spa-Bench, DiVeR and the second halves of 2609.39971, CRAFT and VLA-Trace were not read. ICLR 2027 OpenReview submissions were not searched. Quoted numbers should be re-checked against the PDFs before they are cited.

## Three 2026 preprints already describe "where breaks, how survives"

The empirical split between intact manipulation and wrong grounding is established and must be cited, not claimed. ReGuide (Zhang et al., 29 Sep 2026) states that VLAs "fail at global grounding while retaining local manipulation skills" and "exploit visual shortcuts" ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38616)). Its full text goes further than the abstract. **Eq. 1 factors success into a binding term β and a competence term γ**, and the method is described as "semantic rebinding" plus "geometric rebinding" ([ReGuide HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). This is the nearest collision with the theory: ReGuide already uses binding vocabulary and a two-factor decomposition. The difference is the level of the decomposition. ReGuide factors a *success probability*. The proposed theory factors the *action distribution* into a mixture over role-to-entity assignments, and it adds claims about the form of each factor (P exemplar-keyed, O equivariant). ReGuide makes neither claim.

"Robust Skills, Brittle Grounding" (Emukpere, Deffayet, Renders) tests SmolVLA and pi0.5 on held-out object-location pairings. It concludes that "manipulation skill acquisition is decoupled from instruction following" ([arXiv 2602.24143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24143)). CAG/LIBERO-CF (Fang et al.) reports that, without scene-specific supervision, VLAs "pick frequently seen objects regardless of the instruction" ([arXiv 2602.17659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17659)). That sentence is the qualitative ancestor of prediction (a).

The most dangerous collision with the *theory* is arXiv 2609.39971, "When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories" (Chen, Hou, …, Sun, Lee; 30 Sep 2026). It covers fine-tuned pi0.5 and GR00T-N1.7, and its findings map almost point for point onto the factorization:

| Finding in 2609.39971 | Part of the theory it touches |
|---|---|
| 69% of 381 pi0.5 rollouts were "retrieval-like" | P is exemplar-keyed |
| "a 10 cm target shift was followed visually" | O tracks the bound object |
| in every combined swap-and-shift probe, the policy tracked the displaced object in the **source-bound slot**, not the instructed object | the wrong filler fills the role |
| "the instruction selects the trajectory family" | binding selects, motion executes |
| replacing the prefix-KV with the target task's state moved pi0.5 toward the target in **109/200** rollouts (0/200 for controls) | binding sits in the prefix cache |

Sources: ([2609.39971](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39971)). That paper names "instruction-action binding," shows filler substitution with intact visual tracking, and moves behaviour with a prefix-KV swap. The theory must be positioned as the formal and mechanistic refinement of this picture, not as a rival to it. Three refinements are open. First, 2609.39971 says the *instruction* selects the family, while the theory and CRAFT say the binding is keyed on the nearest training *scene plus instruction*. Prediction (a) decides between the two readings. Second, its prefix-KV swap moves the whole prefix (image and text together), and C1 splits that prefix into image, region and word positions. Third, it does not compare frames.

CRAFT (Yang et al., 30 Sep 2026) supplies the scene-keyed half of the theory: the visual scene serves as "a proxy for the instruction" ([CRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00524)). Its motivating study reports that pi0.5 executes a demonstrated task sharing the object 38.4% of the time on undemonstrated LIBERO-Goal combinations ([CRAFT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00524)). Li's "Confined yet Capable" calls the effect "spatial overfitting," in which object names map to demonstrated locations ([arXiv 2505.03500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03500)).

Two lines of prior work give the exemplar framing outside VLAs, and the paper should use them. He et al. argue that diffusion policies "essentially memorize an action lookup table" keyed on the nearest training image. Their policies output training action sequences even on OOD images, and an ICLR 2026 poster version exists ([arXiv 2505.05787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05787); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009689)). Dasgupta, Grant and Griffiths show that standard networks are "feature-biased and exemplar-based" ([arXiv 2110.04328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04328)). The theory differs from He et al. in a testable way: the lookup operates over *bindings*, not whole trajectories. To test it, move the training-bound object to a new pose. A lookup table replays the old trajectory. The proposed theory reaches the *new* pose of the *wrong* object, which is what 2609.39971's swap-and-shift probes already suggest. Two results push back on the motor half. AC-VLA attributes pi0.5 failures partly to "trajectory overfitting," i.e. holistic motor patterns ([arXiv 2607.15714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15714)). Grant et al. find that *injected* expert activations are "bound to absolute workspace coordinates" ([arXiv 2603.19233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19233)). C2 is the evidence that answers both.

No checked source models VLA behaviour as an explicit mixture over role-to-entity assignments. HULC and MCIL learn latent *plans* ([HULC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.06252); [MCIL](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p047.pdf)). CAST reports mode oscillation when several behaviours fit the image, which is consistent with a mixture but not modelled as one ([arXiv 2508.13446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13446)). The binding-problem framing is mature for VLMs: Campbell et al. trace multi-object failures to interference in shared representations ([arXiv 2411.00238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00238)), and binding IDs have been reported on objects' image tokens ([arXiv 2505.22200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22200)). Neither framing has been applied to action policies in the sources found.

## Per-claim verdicts: localization, measurement and timing remain open

| Claim | Verdict | Closest collision | How to position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theory (mixture; exemplar P, equivariant O) | **Novel as a formal model; qualitative picture anticipated** | ReGuide Eq. 1 (β·γ); 2609.39971 (retrieval + visual tracking + source-bound slot); CRAFT (scene as proxy) | The action-level refinement that makes graded predictions; discriminate from He et al.'s whole-trajectory lookup |
| C1 scene-wide code in deep image tokens | **Novel** | VLA-Trace knockouts; 2609.39971 prefix-KV swap; 2603.19233 App. G | The localized, causal version of their prefix-level results; rejects the object-local binding-ID hypothesis |
| C2 shared, object-frame operators | **Novel in form** | EquiVLA's ε_eq; 2609.39971's 10 cm tracking; 2603.19233 App. B | Runs the frame control 2603.19233 says it "has not run" |
| C3 commitment at the grasp | **Novel quantitatively** | DiVeR (criticality at pre-grasp); 2605.28527 | The coverage twin of DiVeR's variance result |
| C4 internal handles fail | **Novel as a systematic result; partially conceded** | ReGuide's CAG row; CRAFT's CAG-VA at 66.5% | Coverage argument; run IGAR and TAG by name |
| C5 relational sub-region class | **Novel as a named class** | ReGuide A2 cell (5/50); SpatialActor's anecdote | Cite as corroborating data |
| (a) wrong = most frequent training binding | **Novel quantitatively** | CAG's "frequently seen objects"; CRAFT 38.4% | Per-scene frequency test; credit CAG |
| (b) error rises with relational depth | **Novel for VLAs** | RefSpatial step counts; Spa-Bench categories | Graded type < instance < sub-region ordering on an end-to-end VLA |
| (c) external binding fixes binding, not motion | **Partly anticipated as a method** | ReGuide (+55 points with external grounding) | Claim the dissociation, not the fix |
| Testbed (own pi0.5, held out at weights) | **Not novel per se** | CRAFT | LIBERO-90 object × goal × scene split; first held-out-at-weights test of ReGuide and of internals |

**C1.** The nearest prior is VLA-Trace on pi0.5 ([arXiv 2605.30117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30117)). Removing text during generation barely hurts LIBERO-Goal (96.0 to 96.5). Blocking vision-language attention during prefill collapses Goal to 11.5. Removing the image during generation zeroes most suites ([VLA-Trace HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30117)). Together these *imply* that instruction content reaches the action pathway through the visual tokens, but VLA-Trace never states it. It also splices nothing and does not separate region from whole-image positions. The paper has internal inconsistencies: a 93.5 vs 75.0 baseline, and a "1.3 points" text claim that conflicts with its table.

Two other papers patch the action expert, never the image tokens. Grant et al. inject only at the 50 action-token positions in expert layers 16–17 ([arXiv 2603.19233](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19233)). Wulff and Cangelosi patch the action-expert residual offline, on training data, find pi0.5 sensitivity in early layers, and report that attribution does not predict activation patching for pi0.5 (r = 0.089) ([arXiv 2610.10178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10178)).

The VLM literature mostly finds the opposite flow, from image into text and question tokens in the middle layers ([arXiv 2411.18620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18620)). Binding-ID work predicts an object-local code ([arXiv 2505.22200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22200)). C1's inert region splice therefore directly tests and rejects the object-local hypothesis for this policy. The likely reason is pi0.5's bidirectional prefix, which lets image tokens read the prompt. That explanation is our inference and is not shown in any cited paper.

C1 has two tensions to defuse. First, 2603.19233 App. G restores goal selection with edits at the instruction positions in layers 2 and 5 (113/150) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19233)). That restores a trained, uncontested goal, not a held-out binding, but a reviewer will raise it against "binding leaves the words by layer 2." Second, the 2609.39971 prefix-KV result means C1 must present its splice as a *decomposition* of a known effect, not as the first KV intervention.

**C2.** No source measures a per-observation displacement gain, a type-shared gain, or a frame comparison in a pretrained VLA. EquiVLA is the only metric-level precedent. It measures rotation equivariance error on GR00T N1.5 under C8 image rotations (ε_eq = 7.754) as a motivation for building equivariance in ([EquiVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19784)). It covers rotation only, uses synthetic image transforms, and reports an error norm, not a gain.

Grant et al. look like a contradiction ("bound to absolute workspace coordinates"). Their own control dissolves it. "Without injection and with the source prompt, the policy reaches the displaced object." Their App. B also states that a rigid whole-world transform "would separate the two, and we have not run it" ([arXiv 2603.19233](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19233)). C2's leave-scene-out transfer (0.62 in the object frame, −0.07 in the world frame) is exactly that missing control. The paper should quote their sentence and present the result as completing their analysis.

LIBERO-PRO and LIBERO-Plus report collapse under position changes and call it "rote memorization" or "positional bias" ([LIBERO-PRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03827); [LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626v1)). These are task-success results under large shifts, and they are compatible with a gain of 0.13–0.25. The operator tracks the object, but only partly. The paper should define equivariance "up to gain" before a reviewer calls the operator a mere attraction. MCF-Proto composes actions from prototypes in a learned local frame, which is a *design* analogue, but it measures nothing about pretrained models ([arXiv 2605.11809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11809)). Post-hoc primitive work, such as SAE motion features ([arXiv 2603.19183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19183)) and behavioural-mode discovery ([arXiv 2605.11387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11387)), reports no shared operator parameters across object types.

**C3.** No source measures referent commitment or per-phase coverage. DiVeR finds that sample divergence on pi0 and pi0.5 is "sparse and temporally localized" at pre-grasp and pre-placement states, with routine behaviour during post-grasp transport. It gives no per-phase numbers and does not frame the result as referent binding ([DiVeR](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04933v1)). C3's 0.53 vs 0.20 coverage@16 adds the stronger statement that the *correct* referent is present in the samples before the grasp and leaves them afterwards. That is commitment, not just shrinking variance. The 2605.28527 probe paper says "the better continuations were already inside the sampling distribution," but gives no coverage and no timing. Its selector also uses privileged simulator-reset lookahead ([arXiv 2605.28527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28527)). Grant et al. localize pi0.5's steering sensitivity to the transport phase and report no grasp-phase analysis ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19233)).

## C4 is new as a system, but CAG and CRAFT already hold pieces

No source tests a battery of policy-internal handles on a split held out at the weights. Every existing internal handle reports positive results, but each on a different failure type:

| Handle | Failure type it targets | Result reported | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| IGAR attention recalibration | Contradictory instructions | Positive on 30 LIBERO tasks | [arXiv 2603.06001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06001) |
| TAG object-erased CFG | Clutter | Positive on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, VLABench | [arXiv 2603.24584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24584) |
| FFN steering | Behaviour modulation | Positive | [arXiv 2509.00328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00328) |
| SAE feature steering | Behaviour modulation | Positive | [arXiv 2603.19183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19183) |
| Critic-guided flow sampling | Nominal tasks and perturbations | 60.0% to 82.5% | [arXiv 2607.02092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02092) |

None of these targets recombination, so none contradicts C4. Reviewers will still ask for IGAR and TAG to be run by name, not only through the nearest analogues (early attention steering and contrastive region guidance). One out-of-domain result supports C4: in a driving VLA, hooking only the first 8 layers with an attention bias moves trajectories about 2.0 cm, against about 67.6 cm for all 36 layers ([arXiv 2608.17095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17095)).

Two concessions are required. In ReGuide's Table 1, CAG raises public pi0.5's composition average only from 39.0 to 41.4 and cuts original-task success from 94.0 to 81.4 ([ReGuide HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). That is a prior failure of one handle, so C4 cannot claim it first. CRAFT is a partial counterexample. Its CAG-VA reaches **66.5%** undemonstrated on Pick-Place pi0.5, against 8.4% for full fine-tuning, while scoring 1–7% for pi0 and GR00T and 21.8% on Pick-Place-Press ([CRAFT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00524)). C4 must explain that number. Three explanations are available: a two-attribute colour split is shallower in relational depth, which is prediction (b) at work; CAG-VA uses a separately *trained* vision-action branch; and its guidance strength was tuned per benchmark. ReGuide has also already stated C4's thesis qualitatively: steering that amplifies the policy's own language-dependent distribution "cannot carry the end-effector to a referent it never considered." So C4 is the systematic, measured version of an argument already in print.

Verifier and selection methods all report gains on OOD perturbations or nominal tasks, never on held-out recombinations or region binding. These include V-GPS ([arXiv 2410.13816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13816)), RoboMonkey ([arXiv 2506.17811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17811)), CoVer ([arXiv 2602.12281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12281)) and VERITAS ([arXiv 2606.18247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18247)). C3 supplies the mechanism behind C4's verifier result, and that mechanism is a contribution in its own right. Selection is capped by coverage. Coverage of the right binding is 0.53 before the grasp and 0.20 after it, and a first-chunk verifier reads geometry that does not yet encode the choice.

## Relational depth and sub-region errors have neighbours, not owners

For prediction (b), RoboRefer/RefSpatial grades spatial referring by reasoning steps (up to 5) for a *pointing VLM* ([NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/29416b66c2149872b9d1415a3fd2c5e0-Abstract-Conference.html)). Spa-Bench (8 Oct 2026) tests ordinal, relational and referential categories on real-robot VLAs, with success falling from 67.5–73.3% in distribution to 40.7–52.0% out of distribution ([arXiv 2610.12696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12696)). Neither orders type < instance < relational sub-region for an end-to-end VLA, and the Spa-Bench full text, which may define failure classes, was not read. Campbell et al.'s interference account predicts that error grows with the number of *similar entities* ([arXiv 2411.00238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00238)). The proposed theory predicts that error grows with relational *depth* and tracks training frequency. Presenting the two as competing accounts, and holding entity count fixed while varying depth, turns (b) from a curve into a discriminating test.

For C5, the strongest corroboration comes from a competitor's own data. In ReGuide's Table 9, bare public pi0.5 succeeds **5/50** on "put the cream cheese in the top drawer of the wooden cabinet" (A2) and 42/50 on the black-bowl variant (A3). ReGuide does not analyse these cells as a failure class, no Target Region cell involves a shelf, and its limitations exclude relational instructions ([ReGuide HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616)). SpatialActor mentions opening "the wrong drawer" as an anecdote ([arXiv 2511.09555](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09555)). Cite both as precedent data for a class that nobody has named.

On the testbed: CRAFT already fine-tunes pi0, pi0.5 and GR00T only on demonstrated combinations of LIBERO-derived Pick-Place splits, with 4 of 16 cube × plate combinations demonstrated ([CRAFT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00524)). "Own pi0.5, held out at the weights" is therefore a precedented design. It differentiates the paper sharply from ReGuide, Grant et al., Wulff and Cangelosi, VLA-Trace, 2609.39971 and the probe paper, all of which use public suite-trained checkpoints on training tasks or recombinations of them. Two things are distinct about this testbed. The split is an object × goal × scene split built on LIBERO-90, not a colour split. And ReGuide's external-binding result has never been shown on a policy where the combination is unseen at the weights, so running it there would be a new result.

## What a binding-focused solution must do to stand apart

The prior methods divide the design space cleanly, and that leaves a specific gap:

| Method | Mechanism | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| ReGuide | Supplies the binding externally and overrides the policy's actions | Oracle or detector-plus-depth poses; destination-class pre-contact statistics from training demos; a bounded controller that replaces the policy's commands, then hands back (86.0% with perception vs 96.0% oracle on Target Region) |
| CRAFT | Fixes binding by fine-tuning | Counterfactual instruction pairs built from existing demos |
| CAG, TAG | Contrast against a language-dropped or object-erased branch | A null branch; CAG-VA needs a trained vision-action policy |
| IGAR | Pushes attention toward the instruction tokens | Access to attention heads |
| Verifiers (V-GPS, RoboMonkey, CoVer, VERITAS, DiVeR, frozen-feature probes) | Select among the policy's own samples | A trained verifier, a VLM, or simulator lookahead |

Sources: ([ReGuide](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38616); [CRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00524); [CAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17659); [TAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24584); [IGAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06001); [VERITAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18247); [DiVeR](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04933v1)).

The theory says where each of these falls short. CAG, TAG and IGAR act on the language channel or on attention to words, but C1 says binding has left the words by layer 2 and lives in deep image tokens. Verifiers are capped by coverage, which C3 shows collapses after the grasp. ReGuide works but replaces O with an external controller during transport. CRAFT works but changes the weights.

A binding method is novel against all of them if it meets five requirements. It acts on P alone and leaves O untouched, and shows this with the prediction-(c) dissociation (binding errors fixed, motion errors not). It intervenes where the theory puts the binding, in the deep image-token code, or retargets the policy's *own* operator through its measured object-frame equivariance, rather than transporting the end-effector with an external controller. It acts in the pre-grasp window that C3 identifies. It detects errors without a learned verifier, using prediction (a): if the predicted chunk endpoint lands on the scene's most frequent training binding, flag the episode. It handles relational sub-regions, which ReGuide's destination-class statistics and scope exclude.

Two collisions set the bar. Whole-prefix KV replacement is already in 2609.39971 (109/200). The user's own oracle-retrieval splice closes only 0.21 of the gap. So "splice a retrieved exemplar's image K/V" is neither novel nor sufficient by itself. The open directions are operator-level retargeting and deep-image-code editing driven by the instructed role. Operator-level retargeting means mapping the wrong-bound chunk through the relative object-frame pose of the instructed entity, with the gain of 0.13–0.25 calibrated and not assumed to be 1. Deep-image-code editing means writing the instructed role into the scene code, not pulling in a retrieved scene wholesale.

Whatever supplies the *identity* of the instructed entity has to be honest about its resources. If it uses a VLM point, as VERITAS and RoboRefer do, its only claim to novelty over ReGuide is that the policy composes the motion itself instead of an external controller supplying it. It must be compared against ReGuide's perception-grounded number, not the oracle one.

## Conclusion

The defensible contribution is a mechanistic account that turns a widely reported observation into falsifiable structure. It locates the binding (C1), shows the operator is shared and object-relative (C2), times the commitment (C3), and makes graded predictions (a) and (b) that the shortcut and lookup-table accounts do not make. The first-pass binding-literature notes under-weighted two things. ReGuide's full text already uses binding vocabulary and a β·γ factorization. And 2609.39971 independently found retrieval plus visual tracking plus a KV-movable binding in pi0.5 a month earlier. Prediction (a) is therefore the paper's sharpest weapon. It distinguishes scene-keyed exemplar binding (this theory, CRAFT) from instruction-selected trajectory families (2609.39971) and from whole-trajectory lookup (He et al.), and none of those papers ran it.

The most informative next experiments are four. The first is the prediction-(a) frequency test per scene. The second runs IGAR and TAG by name on the held-out split. The third runs 2603.19233's injection protocol together with its unrun rigid-transform control on this checkpoint. The fourth tests whether a pre-grasp, operator-level retarget beats ReGuide's perception-grounded number without overriding the policy. Until the ReGuide, CRAFT, CAG, TAG, IGAR and Spa-Bench full texts are read, and ICLR 2027 submissions are checked, every "novel" verdict above is provisional.
