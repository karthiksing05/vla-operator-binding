Out-of-Distribution Learning Is Composition: A Supervised and an Unsupervised Scheme for Frozen Flow-Matching Policies

pi0.5, pi0, SmolVLA, X-VLA, GR00T N1.5 and FLOWER on LIBERO-90, Li et al.'s compositional OOD suites and LIBERO-PRO · the text is paper/DRAFT.md, every number audited against the project's ledger at commit ba21f956.

Abstract

A frozen flow-matching vision-language-action policy fails most new tasks, although it can reach, grasp, carry and place. We argue that learning such tasks out of distribution is composition: a new task is a new arrangement of parts and relations the policy already holds or can learn from one example, and the work lies in finding those parts, storing them cheaply and recombining them. We grade a compositional split of LIBERO by what each test task leaves unseen (a relation, a binding, a scene or a part) and test the claim with two schemes on one framework. The supervised scheme reads a task's concepts (object, instance, goal) from its instruction and switches them at the policy's own grasp; a concept the policy holds is a zero-byte pointer to its own field under a training instruction, a new one is a fitted rank-4 prototype or task leaf, the policy's likelihood decides which (AUC 1.0, a detector Wang et al. leave to future work), and practice chooses sources, operators and what to keep. The unsupervised scheme cuts a demonstration at its contact events, names each unit by the policy's likelihood and serves pointer programs or a soft blend of held fields. The framework needs only access to a policy's prototypes and runs unchanged on five architectures. Where only relations or bindings are new and the scene is familiar, reuse wins on all five: on Li's compositional OOD suites, with no demonstration of any test task, pi0 rises from 35 to 336 of 400 and pi0.5 from 143 to 312; SmolVLA rises from 5 to 96 and X-VLA from 80 to 256; GR00T N1.5 rises from 113 to 157. The system is level with or above a prior method's text-latent interpolation with hand-chosen task pairs on every one (316 and 245 on pi0 and pi0.5; 0 and 142 on SmolVLA and X-VLA; 61 on GR00T N1.5). No fixed operator carries across architectures, and practice picks the operator per task. Where the scene or a part is new, nothing reused helps (on LIBERO-90, held fields composed with nothing fitted: 233 of 500 against 245 for the policy alone) and created units do: task leaves kept by practice beat a separate adapter per task (994 against 932 of 1,320; difference 0.047, 95% interval 0.008 to 0.091) with 675.9 MB against 902.8 MB. Learned as a stream, the library requests a demonstration only when practice fails and damages no earlier task; one adapter trained through the same stream by sequential fine-tuning or experience replay reaches 52 or 335 of 600 against 465. The likelihood names units but does not cut them, and what a held field donates follows behaviour, not concept names.

1Introduction

  • Thesis: out-of-distribution learning on a frozen policy is a composition problem. The policy already carries most of a new task as concepts it learned under other instructions; what it lacks is the arrangement. We show it on five architectures with one framework whose only architectural assumption is access to the policy's prototypes (Section 4).
  • The grade of novelty decides the regime. A test task is graded by which parts and relations it leaves unseen (Section 5). New relations and bindings in familiar scenes are learned by reuse; new scenes and new parts need units to be created.
  • Robotics is an ideal testbed for compositionality. A manipulation task is an explicit composition of parts (an object, its location, a goal relation and its location, a scene), each part has a measurable effect on the motion, and success is checked by a simulator rather than by a judge. Prior knowledge here donates expertise in unorthodox ways: a field trained to put one object in a basket carries the approach to another. This is the systematicity Fodor asked of minds and the task-to-task transfer that cognitive psychology documents in people.
  • Scope: flow-matching VLAs, whose action expert integrates a learned velocity field (pi0, pi0.5, SmolVLA, X-VLA, GR00T N1.5; FLOWER on its single suite). Every unit of the framework is a change to that field for one phase of a step, which makes composition a matter of choosing, per phase, which field the policy integrates. Our companion ICLR-2027 submission finds the same split for image diffusion: composing the scores of single-object prompts places objects that one prompt naming them all does not.
  • The failure mode: in familiar scenes the policy runs a trained routine on the wrong object; on the 6 tasks only a per-task adapter solves, it grasps the wrong object first in 114 of 120 episodes (Section 7).
  • Two schemes. The supervised scheme reads concepts from the instruction and is built as one system, iterated: each iteration is a measured weakness of the previous version and one change motivated by it, pre-registered and tested on start states no earlier decision saw (Section 7). The unsupervised scheme takes its units from the demonstration alone (Section 8).
  • Lineage: Wang et al. (arXiv) select and compose a frozen generative model's prototypes for an out-of-distribution query; their stated gap is a mechanism to detect, consolidate and reuse new primitives. We supply one for an embodied flow policy: likelihood detects a new concept, and practice, not likelihood, selects among concepts.
  • Contributions (Limitations states the limits):
    1. a compositional split graded by unseen parts and relations (G0 seen, G1 relation, G2 binding, G3 scene, G4 part), and the finding that the grade predicts whether a task needs reuse (G1, G2) or creation (G3, G4);
    2. a compositional library for a frozen flow-matching VLA whose units are instruction concepts (object type, instance as a child of its type, goal), each a low-rank change to the action expert fitted on the frames of its phase pooled over every demonstration of the concept, switched at the policy's own grasp; skill-level adapter banks composed by a learned router exist (PRISM), but we know of no prior work that factors a VLA's adapters into object and goal concepts;
    3. for concepts the policy already holds, a zero-byte unit: the policy's own field under a training instruction, switched at the grasp. This extends Li's prompt switching (hand-paired base tasks, a fixed switching step, pi0): practice chooses the sources and, per task, the operator (grasp switch or Li's text-latent interpolation, ported to every policy's conditioning encoder) with no hand labels, level with or above Li's hand-chosen pairs on all five policies; no single operator wins on every architecture, so the choice by practice is what makes the system architecture-agnostic;
    4. the frozen policy's own flow-matching loss on a concept's phase frames as the decision to point or to fit, with no added parameters (earlier continual learners decide expansion with separate models, e.g. CLARE's feature similarity), and the finding that likelihood detects novelty but does not select among new concepts; a continual learner that asks for a demonstration only when practice fails and refits every concept on its path (cf. LOTUS, which updates and adds skills);
    5. an unsupervised scheme with no instruction grammar: units cut at contact events and named by likelihood, served as pointer programs or as a soft blend of held fields, with a donation analysis of what each held field contributes;
    6. the iteration record itself: each failure and the change it motivated, with negative results that scope the claim (query-likelihood selection among new concepts, demonstration-free transfer to new objects, prototypes of held concepts, likelihood-placed unit boundaries);
    7. a pre-registered, paired, audited protocol on fresh start states, and a defect found and fixed in a public LIBERO-90 port.

2Related work

Composition of generative models (Composable Diffusion, Liu et al., ECCV 2022, arXiv) and of fine-tuned weights (task arithmetic, Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv); test-time prototype discovery (Wang et al., arXiv); flow-matching vision-language-action policies (pi0, arXiv; pi0.5, CoRL 2025, arXiv; SmolVLA, arXiv; GR00T N1, arXiv; FLOWER, CoRL 2025, arXiv; X-VLA, arXiv); adapting a policy from few demonstrations by retrieval (STRAP, ICLR 2025, arXiv) and by reinforcement learning in its noise space (DSRL, CoRL 2025, arXiv); continual learning with frozen backbones and per-task modules (PackNet, Piggyback, SupSup, C-LoRA; docs/RELATED.md); the benchmarks (LIBERO, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv; Li's compositional OOD suites, arXiv).

Continual imitation with module libraries. Per-task adapters on a frozen pretrained policy avoid forgetting (TAIL, ICLR 2024, arXiv; our per-task LoRA baseline is of this kind); later work shares modules across tasks with learned routers (Sparse Diffusion Policy, CoRL 2024, arXiv; DMPEL, arXiv), grows libraries of discovered skills (LOTUS, ICRA 2024, arXiv; IsCiL, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv), or expands adapters only where needed (CLARE, RA-L 2026, arXiv); PRISM (NeurIPS 2026) routes frozen low-rank skill adapters on the action expert per chunk, and PhaseLoRA (arXiv) conditions one action-expert LoRA on the manipulation phase. Our units are instruction concepts or contact segments, not tasks or skills, switched by the policy's own gripper events, not a learned router, and include zero-byte pointers to the policy's own conditionals. Pretrained VLAs forget little under fine-tuning with replay (arXiv). Compositional generalisation of VLAs. On the suites we use, Li switches pi0's prompt mid-episode and interpolates its text latents; ACT-VLA (arXiv) and AC-VLA (arXiv) retrain pi0.5 on synthesised or decomposed compositional demonstrations, training-based references for our frozen policy. Composition of diffusion and flow policies (PoCo, RSS 2024, arXiv; GPC, ICLR 2026, arXiv) combines whole policies at every step; we keep one policy frozen and switch its conditionals per phase, and we compare with Li's operators on the same tasks (G4d). Novelty in generative policies. Denoising error detects out-of-distribution inputs (Graham et al., CVPR-W 2023, arXiv); flow-based densities flag failing policy rollouts (FAIL-Detect, RSS 2025, arXiv); interactive imitation requests help on novel states (ThriftyDAgger, arXiv). We use the policy's own flow loss per concept to decide whether a concept needs new parameters.

3The policies: flow-matching vision-language-action models

Every policy we test has the same three parts, and the framework touches only the interfaces between them.

  1. A vision-language encoder reads the camera images and the instruction (and, for some, the robot's state) once per query. Its output is the conditioning: a cache of keys and values, or a sequence of features.
  2. An action expert maps a noisy action chunk x_t, a flow time t and the conditioning to a velocity v(x_t, t). It is trained by flow matching on the straight path x_t = t·ε + (1 − t)·a between noise ε and a demonstrated chunk a, with target u = ε − a. Its velocity defines a field over chunks of future actions, so every identity we use holds on every policy: E[a | x_t] = x_t − t·v, and the score is −E[ε | x_t] / t.
  3. A sampler integrates the field from noise to a chunk in a few steps. The robot executes the start of the chunk and the policy is queried again (our client replans every 5 actions on every policy).

Two attention styles are present:

  • joint: the expert attends to the encoder's cache inside shared layers (pi0, pi0.5, SmolVLA);
  • cross: a separate DiT or transformer reads the encoder's output (X-VLA, GR00T N1.5, FLOWER).

Two output types are present: velocity and clean-chunk prediction. Both define the same field, since v = (x_t − â) / t.

What the framework reads from a policy (lifelong_ood/policy/backbone*.py, one wrapper per policy, one interface):

  • the conditioning under any instruction: a held concept is the policy's own field under a training instruction (the grasp switch, pointer programs, the soft blend);
  • the encoder's residual stream at the instruction's tokens, through a hook before each encoder layer: text-latent interpolation and the word splice;
  • the velocity at any (x_t, t): the flow-matching loss that decides held vs new, and the per-dimension blend;
  • a low-rank adapter on the action expert: the fitted prototypes and task leaves.

No operator needs more than these four, which is why one framework runs unchanged on all six policies (five architectures).

4The framework

(docs/METHOD.md, docs/UNSUPERVISED.md; Figures: one composed step; the concept hierarchy; the stream learner.)

Units. A unit is a change to the policy's velocity field for one phase of a task. The two schemes differ only in where the units come from:

  • supervised: object, instance and goal concepts are read from the instruction; an instance is a child of its object type, and the object (or instance) unit acts until the policy's grasp, the goal unit after it;
  • unsupervised: the demonstration is cut at its own contact events (gripper close and open), and each segment is a unit.

Held or new, by likelihood. A unit is scored by the frozen policy's own flow-matching loss on its frames (a detector of the kind Wang et al. leave to future work). A unit the policy holds under its instruction and scene is stored as a pointer to the policy's own field under the training instruction that best explains it (zero bytes); a new unit is fitted as a prototype, a rank-4 LoRA on the action expert fitted by flow matching on the frames of its phase in every demonstration of the concept, each demonstration from the task's own scene. Below the shared concepts, a task they cannot serve keeps its own one-demonstration adapter as a task leaf.

Composition at the grasp. The object (or instance) unit is on until the policy's gripper stays closed for three queries; the goal unit then acts until the release. The learner thus composes held concepts the way composable diffusion composes the scores of single prompts, and grows the library only where the policy's likelihood says it must. A pointer program generalises the switch: a list of training instructions, advanced online at the policy's own gripper events.

Operators for held units. A held unit points at a source training instruction, and three operators read the policy through it. The grasp switch conditions the policy on the object's source instruction until the grasp and on the goal's source instruction after it. Text-latent interpolation (Li et al.) keeps the test instruction and shifts the residual stream of the policy's conditioning encoder, at the instruction's tokens, from the object source's latent toward the goal source's. The soft blend mixes the policy's fields under several training instructions convexly, per segment and per action dimension.

Practice and the stream. Ten practice episodes keep the composition only if it beats the policy, and practice chooses each concept's source and, per task, the operator. As a stream, an arrival requests its one demonstration only when neither the policy nor the composition reaches 8 of 10; the demonstration refits the instance, type and goal concepts it belongs to.

What the framework needs from a policy. Nothing in the framework depends on one architecture. It needs three accesses to a frozen flow-matching policy: its velocity field under a given instruction (a pointer, the grasp switch and the blend), a low-rank change to that field (a prototype), and the residual stream of the encoder that reads the instruction, with the position of the instruction's tokens in it (text-latent interpolation). These are the extraction of prototypes. One interface supplies them for PaliGemma (pi0, pi0.5), SmolVLM (SmolVLA), Florence-2 (X-VLA), Eagle (GR00T N1.5) and for FLOWER; the rest of the framework is shared code.

5The compositional split

(docs/SPLIT.md; experiments/split_grades.py.) A LIBERO task is a composition of parts in a scene. The training set is the 40 tasks every policy we test was fine-tuned on (LIBERO-Spatial, Object, Goal and LIBERO-10), so we do not choose it. Parts are read by rule from each task's BDDL file, with one normalisation applied to every task alike:

  • object: the moved object's type;
  • object location: its instance qualifier;
  • goal relation: the placing predicate and target type;
  • goal location: the region, when it names a location such as a drawer or a side;
  • scene: the layout. Li's OOD suites use their base suite's table, with objects added.

Following Zekun Wang's criterion ("a set with parts and relations unseen"), every test task is graded by what it leaves unseen. The grade measures how far prior knowledge must be bent to serve a new task.

gradeunseenexample
G0 seennothing: the (object, relation) pair in this scene is a training taskLIBERO-90 copies of LIBERO-10 steps
G1 relationthe pairing only: object and goal each seen in this scene, never togetherLi's "put the cream cheese on the plate"
G2 bindinga part never seen in this scene, though seen elsewhereLi's "put the butter on the plate" (butter only in LIBERO-Object)
G3 scenethe scene; every part seen in other scenesLIBERO-90 kitchens and living rooms not in LIBERO-10
G4 partan object, goal relation or predicate never seen at allLIBERO-90's ketchup in the tray, the caddy, the frying pan

Counts over the 110 evaluated tasks: G0 15; G1 9; G2 20; G3 18; G4 48. Li's 20 compositional OOD tasks are G1 or G2; the LIBERO-90 tasks span G0 to G4. Section 10 reports every result by grade.

6Protocol

Pre-registered before any episode; paired episodes (same start state and noise for every arm); fresh start states from LIBERO's own placement sampler at unused seeds; exact sign test on discordant pairs, two-level bootstrap over tasks and episodes; non-inferiority margin 0.05. The strong baseline is a rank-16 LoRA per task fitted on the task's own scene's demonstration and given the same acceptance test.

7The supervised scheme: iterations of one system

Each iteration names the weakness the previous version showed, the one change, and the pre-registered result. Figure (figures/robot/iterations.png) summarises the ladder: on LIBERO-90 the system against the per-task adapter at iterations 1-3 and 8, and on Li's suites the policy alone, word-matched, hand-chosen and practice-chosen sources. Iterations 1-4, 6 and 8 work on LIBERO-90, where most tasks are G3 or G4; iterations 5 and 9-14 work on Li's suites (G1, G2); iteration 7 joins the two.

Iteration 1: goal units after the grasp

Weakness: the frozen policy fails most new tasks. Change: one goal prototype per goal concept, acting from the grasp. Result: kept 343 against 271 zero-shot, but 324 against 454 for the per-task adapter. The approach is where it fails (114 of 120 wrong first grasps).

Iteration 2: object units before the grasp

Change: an object prototype for the approach. Result, on the corrected data: the factored library keeps 439 against 272 zero-shot and 420 against 478 for the per-task adapter, with 45 of the 58 missing successes on four tasks that name an instance of the black bowl.

Iteration 3: a hierarchy of concepts

Change: instance children under object types (screen: 37 of 40 against 13 for the type prototype). Confirmatory (E3, unseen start states): 499 against 273 zero-shot; 480 against 494 for the per-task adapter with acceptance (difference -0.024, 95% interval -0.074 to 0.024); the instance children 74 of 80 against 17 for the type prototype. Replication on 37 new tasks: 451 of 740 against 151 zero-shot; 451 against 441 for the per-task adapter with acceptance. Pooled over 66 tasks the library and the per-task adapter are level (931 against 935 of 1,320; difference -0.003, 95% interval -0.063 to 0.058); non-inferiority at 0.05 is just missed (the lower bound misses it by 0.013).

Iteration 4: learning as a stream

Change: a task asks for its one demonstration only when practice fails (W1). Result: 17 and 16 demonstrations in two orders (21 and 20 under a looser rule), no damage to earlier arrivals in either order, served 465 and 496 of 600 against 273 zero-shot.

Iteration 5: concepts the policy already holds

Weakness: on Li's compositional OOD suites (arXiv; 20 tasks, each pairing one training task's object with another's target, no demonstration of any test task), prototypes fitted on pi0.5's own training demonstrations learn nothing (flow loss 0.0024 at the start, 0.0038 at the end, against 0.060 to 0.0062 on LIBERO-90 demonstrations), and the kept arms equal the policy (154 against 157 of 400). Change: a held concept is the policy's own field under the instruction of a training task that taught it, switched at the grasp. Result: with practice choosing, the kept arms succeed 174 of 400 times against 147 for the policy (discordant pairs 30 to 3; task-level difference 0.068, 95% interval 0.007 to 0.145), in both suites. This is score composition in the sense of our ICLR-2027 companion: the policy carries each concept under the instruction that taught it, and the composed step reads them one phase at a time. The same composition, unchanged, lifts pi0 from 35 to 98 and SmolVLA from 8 to 25 of 400 on the same tasks (both pass under Holm). The operator is Li's prompt switching, which reproduces on our harness: with Li's hand-chosen source pairs, pi0 succeeds 292 of 400 times switched at their fixed step and 305 switched at its own grasp, against 86 and 74 with our automatically chosen sources (pi0.5: 218 and 222 against 157 and 160). The gap lies entirely in the sources: Li's pairs pick training tasks whose trained motion reaches where the new object lies, ours match the instruction's words. The grasp trigger matches the tuned fixed step without per-task tuning. On X-VLA the composition with practice lifts 75 to 120 of 400.

Iteration 6: the likelihood decides the kind of unit

Weakness: two kinds of unit need a rule to choose between them. Change: the frozen policy's flow-matching loss on a concept's frames. It separates the 34 concepts of its own training tasks from the 40 LIBERO-90 concepts it never saw without overlap (AUC 1.0; held at most 0.0082, new at least 0.026), and the same words are new in new scenes (the cream cheese 0.039 against 0.0040). Likelihood detects novelty although it does not select among new concepts (W0). Exploratory; the held concepts are scored on their training demonstrations. The routing is right in both directions: on 20 LIBERO-90 tasks whose concepts share words with held ones, the field composition equals the policy (88 against 85 of 200) and the prototypes the router assigns reach 173. A text detector that matches a concept's words to held concepts would route 16 of these 40 new concepts to a pointer (28 under the content-word rule); the likelihood routes none.

Iteration 7: one learner, both kinds of task

On a joint stream of 50 arrivals (Li's 20 OOD tasks, which have no demonstrations, and W1's 30 LIBERO-90 tasks), one learner with both kinds of unit requests 17 and 16 demonstrations, all for LIBERO-90 concepts, and serves 641 and 672 of 1,000 against 420 for the policy alone. Pointers carry no weights, so they change no request and damage nothing.

Iteration 8: task leaves

Weakness: the system stays level with, not above, a per-task adapter. Change: below type and instance, a task the shared concepts cannot serve keeps its own one-demonstration adapter as a leaf, kept only where practice prefers it. Result, on a new set of start states over the 66 tasks: 994 of 1,320 against 932 for the per-task adapter with acceptance (discordant pairs 100 to 38; task-level difference 0.047, 95% interval 0.008 to 0.091), superior at the pre-registered criterion, with a leaf on 24 tasks where the per-task baseline keeps an adapter on 48 and with 675.9 MB against 902.8 MB.

Iteration 9: practice chooses the sources

Weakness: the word-matching source rule trails Li's hand-chosen pairs. Change: practice chooses each held concept's source among the 16 single-step pick-and-place training instructions of LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-Spatial (object source first, then goal source, 5 episodes per candidate), with no hand labels and no privileged state. Result, on new start states: on pi0.5 the chosen pairs succeed 269 of 400 times against 222 for Li's hand-chosen pairs at the grasp (task-level difference 0.117, 95% interval 0.022 to 0.240), 160 for our word rule and 147 for the policy alone; on pi0 292 against 305 for Li's pairs (difference -0.033, 95% interval -0.138 to 0.030; level with Li's fixed-step pi0^S at 292), against 74 for the word rule and 35 for the policy. Li's text-latent interpolation (TLI) is the stronger operator on pi0. We port it to the PyTorch pi0 and identify its latents by Li's own procedure; on the same start states it succeeds 325 of 400 times against 35 for the policy alone (Li publish 0.83 in their setup), and it beats the practice-chosen pairs switched at the grasp (292; discordant pairs 76 to 43; the task-level difference is -0.083 with a 95% interval from -0.237 to 0.060). Non-inferiority to TLI fails. TLI still uses Li's hand-chosen pairs and a hand-set interpolation length per task; iteration 10 tests TLI with the practice-chosen pairs.

Iteration 10: TLI with practice-chosen pairs

Weakness: TLI needs hand-chosen pairs. Change: TLI takes the pairs practice chose in iteration 9 and one interpolation length (24) for every task. Result, on new start states (set d): TLI with the chosen pairs falls behind on both policies. On pi0 it succeeds 251 of 400 times against 326 for Li's TLI and 287 for the same pairs switched at the grasp (policy alone 38); on pi0.5, 178 against 259 and 264 (policy alone 155). Pairs that practice chose for the grasp switch do not carry over to interpolation. The two policies differ in which operator wins: on pi0 Li's TLI leads the grasp switch with practice-chosen pairs (difference -0.098, 95% interval -0.263 to 0.055), and on pi0.5 the grasp switch is level with it on set d (difference 0.013, 95% interval -0.170 to 0.182) and ahead on set c (269 against 240; sign p 0.011). Iteration 11 lets practice choose the pairs under TLI.

Iteration 11: practice chooses the pairs under TLI

Change: the practice of iteration 9 runs with TLI as the operator. Result, on set d: on pi0 the chosen pairs succeed 307 of 400 times against 326 for Li's TLI (difference -0.048, 95% interval -0.117 to 0.013) and 251 for TLI with the pairs of iteration 10; on pi0.5, 249 against 259 (difference -0.025, 95% interval -0.142 to 0.092) and 178. Practice recovers most of the gap to hand-chosen pairs without hand labels, and picks Li's exact pair on 11 of 20 tasks for pi0 and 7 of 20 for pi0.5. Non-inferiority to Li's TLI is not established on either policy.

Iteration 12: practice chooses the operator

Weakness: the better operator depends on the policy and the task. Change: per task, 10 practice episodes choose between the grasp switch and TLI, each with its practice-chosen pair; ties go to the switch. Result, on new start states (set e): on pi0 the system succeeds 336 of 400 times against 316 for Li's TLI with hand-chosen pairs (discordant pairs 41 to 21; task-level difference 0.050, 95% interval -0.028 to 0.142), which establishes non-inferiority; on pi0.5, 312 against 245 (difference 0.168, 95% interval 0.058 to 0.290), which is better. The policies alone reach 35 and 143. Practice picks TLI on 8 tasks for pi0 and 5 for pi0.5. No step uses a hand label: the concepts come from the instruction, the sources and the operator from practice.

Iteration 13: practice as a stream

Weakness: iteration 12 practises every task's sources from scratch, even when a concept recurs. Change: Li's 20 tasks arrive as a stream; a held concept's chosen source is stored and reused by later arrivals, so only unseen concepts are practised. Result: practice falls from 6,800 to 3,600 episodes, and success falls with it in three of four streams. Against per-task practice (336 on pi0, 312 on pi0.5 on set e) the streams reach 330 and 288 on pi0 and 254 and 297 on pi0.5 in the two orders; non-inferiority holds only for pi0 in the first order (difference -0.015, 95% interval -0.043 to 0.000). A source chosen for a concept in one task's context does not always serve the same concept with another partner, and the loss depends on which task came first.

Iteration 14: practice gates reuse

Change: the practice gate the system uses everywhere: an arrival whose reused pairs reach fewer than 8 of 10 practice episodes re-practises its reused concepts. Result, on new start states (set f): against per-task practice (334 on pi0, 308 on pi0.5) the gated streams reach 333 and 332 on pi0 and 292 and 309 on pi0.5, with 4,250 to 5,460 practice episodes in place of 6,800. Non-inferiority holds in three of four streams and fails for pi0.5 in the first order (95% lower bound -0.13). Practice that reuses what earlier tasks taught it, checked by practice, keeps the success of practising each task from scratch with fewer practice episodes in every stream.

8The unsupervised scheme

(docs/UNSUPERVISED.md, docs/INTERPRETATION.md section 4.) The supervised scheme needs an instruction grammar to name a task's concepts. The unsupervised scheme takes its units from the demonstration and the policy alone. All reads here are exploratory except Z3 and Z5, which are pre-registered.

Boundaries come from contact events, identities from the likelihood

We record pi0.5's flow loss under its 40 training instructions at every 2nd frame of 117 demos. The likelihood does not find the boundaries. On 72 pick-and-place demos, at about 2 cuts per demo, cuts where the best-explaining instruction switches find the grasp within 10 frames 0.36 of the time against 0.28 for random cuts (release 0.14 against 0.24). Exact 2-cut partitions of the loss signature (0.24) and the prefix latent (0.25) do no better than random 2 cuts (0.30); the demo's arm actions, a non-policy baseline, do (0.58). Per frame, the flow loss is dominated by the observation and the noise draw, not by the instruction. The likelihood does name the units once they are cut at contact events: averaged over a contact segment, the lowest-loss training instruction holds the segment's concept 0.43 (approach) and 0.60 (carry) of the time in familiar scenes, against chance 0.04 and 0.22, and 0.18 and 0.31 against 0.03 and 0.17 in LIBERO-90's scenes. So boundaries come from the demonstration's own contact events (gripper close and open; task-agnostic, no grammar), and identities from the policy's likelihood averaged per segment. In unfamiliar scenes identification is weak: there the policy barely reads its prompt.

Pointer programs

A pointer program is the list of training instructions that name a demonstration's segments, advanced online at the policy's own gripper events, with nothing fitted (lifelong_ood/policy/program.py). The grasp switch is the case where the grammar names the two segments. On the 23 LIBERO-90 tasks the grammar cannot express (drawers, knobs, the microwave, stacking, two-step), programs succeed 43 of 230 times against 37 for the policy alone (exploratory pilot, LIBERO's init states 0-9). Opening the top drawer reaches 6 and 7 against 0 and turning on the stove 9 against 1, but closing the bottom drawer falls to 0 against 10.

9Tests of the system

Does composition generalise?

With each concept refit with no demonstration of a held-out fold (5 folds, 53 eligible tasks), recombined concepts alone succeed 365 of 1,060 times against 354 for the policy: the pre-registered test fails. With practice choosing, 484 against 354. A monolithic adapter trained on the same demonstrations succeeds 190 times, below the policy, so the factorisation matters (365 against 190). Exploratory: on the 33 tasks the policy fails, recombination gives 145 against 25; on the 20 it performs, 220 against 329. Recombination supplies a missing skill and interferes with a present one; practice separates the two.

Other flow-matching policies

The same library refit on SmolVLA, at the same tasks, start states and seed: 203 of 600 against 20 for SmolVLA alone; against SmolVLA's per-task LoRA with acceptance 189 against 274 of 580 (difference -0.147, 95% interval -0.266 to -0.029). On pi0 the library succeeds 324 of 600 times against 85 for pi0 alone; against pi0's per-task LoRA with acceptance, 308 against 330 of 580 (difference -0.038, 95% interval -0.166 to 0.081): non-inferiority is not established on either. X-VLA alone already succeeds 534 of 600 times on these tasks; the kept library 542 (not significant), and 526 against 544 of 580 for X-VLA's per-task LoRA with acceptance: a strong zero-shot policy leaves the library little to add.

Against adapter composition

On 52 of the held-out tasks, summing the per-task adapters of an object donor and a goal donor collapses (71 of 1,040); averaging them stays at the policy's level (328 against 336); the recombined concepts reach 345; with practice they reach 466 against 375 for the better baseline.

Against reinforcement learning in the noise space and retrieval

DSRL steering the frozen policy from 100 practice episodes per task, no demonstration, succeeds 313 of 600 times (314 with acceptance); the library reaches 499 (difference 0.308, 95% interval 0.188 to 0.433). DSRL steering each task's one-demo adapter is the strongest arm we ran: 503 of 580 with acceptance against 480 for the library (difference -0.040, 95% interval -0.098 to 0.012), at ten times the practice and a per-task adapter and actor. DSRL steering the library's composition does not help: 493 against 499 of 600 for the library alone, and 474 against 503 of 580 for DSRL on the per-task adapter. STRAP, retrieving from the library's own data and more, succeeds 192 of 580 alone and 296 with acceptance, against 480 (difference 0.317, 95% interval 0.200 to 0.440).

Against standard continual learning

One rank-16 adapter trained through the same arrival order, one demonstration per task, serves every task at the end, as these methods do (pre-registered G11). Sequential fine-tuning succeeds 52 of 600 times and experience replay 335 against 465 for the stream learner (sign tests p 5e-96 and 6e-17; task-level differences 0.688, 95% interval 0.520 to 0.832, and 0.217, 95% interval 0.077 to 0.353). Sequential fine-tuning falls below the frozen policy (273) and fails every one of the first 10 arrivals (0 successes against 28 on the last 10): the forgetting the library avoids by construction. Experience replay keeps most of the early arrivals (138 on the first 10, 87 on the last 10) but stores every demonstration it replays.

A second benchmark: LIBERO-PRO

LIBERO-PRO (Zhou et al., arXiv) perturbs LIBERO's own suites; its task split gives a training scene a new instruction and success test, a held object paired with a held goal (pre-registered G22; docs/LIBERO_PRO.md). Its benchmark reads each task's instruction from the file name, which the task split leaves unchanged, so the published protocol hands the policy the old instruction. Against other operators for held concepts

Two published demo-free operators, run on the same practice-chosen source pairs as ours so that only the operator varies (pre-registered G21; set e). 11What composition is

(docs/INTERPRETATION.md; Figures: h1_factor_ood.png, h1_donate.png, h1_tli_layers.png, h1_switch.png.)

Composed success is a product of two competences. The grasp is the only interface between a composed task's two phases. The approach unit fixes which object the hand takes: arms that share an object source and differ in goal source take the same first object in 1,803 of 1,805 trial groups on pi0.5. A wrong first grasp ends the task. Composed success is then g x p, the chance that the approach unit grasps the named object times the chance that the carry unit places it. On 53 held-out LIBERO-90 tasks whose units were learned apart, the product predicts 366 successes against 365 observed. On Li's suites, estimated from five practice episodes per source pair on one start-state set, the product predicts the grasp switch on start states practice never saw with Pearson r 0.92 (pi0.5), 0.94 (pi0), 0.91 (SmolVLA) and 0.97 (X-VLA). Units learned together beat the product: on pi0.5's library the hand-off at the grasp adds 0.117 to success after a named grasp, so the product is a lower bound. Composition of held concepts is therefore predictable from its parts, and practice that estimates the parts estimates the whole.

Concepts compose in time, not in superposition. Every operator that runs two concepts at once fails: both prototypes on for the whole episode (489 against 897 of 1,320 when switched), the policy's two fields mixed at every step (61 against 282 of 400 on pi0.5). The same pieces switched at the grasp succeed. The grasp is the interface between a task's concepts, and composition is the choice of which concept acts on each side of it.

Composition is graded for motion and categorical for decisions. Within a phase, a soft blend of held fields beats the best single field on position in 0.78 of familiar demos and 0.84 of LIBERO-90 demos, and on the gripper almost never (0.00 and 0.04). Motion can be mixed; a decision such as closing the hand is taken from one field. The fit-time result says the same (Section 12): a unit must commit to the demonstration's mode.

A prototype corrects where the hand goes, not how it turns. On a held-out demonstration of each concept, an object prototype lowers the position error of the policy's chunk from 1.205 to 0.897 and raises the alignment of the motion with the target from 0.66 to 0.76; a goal prototype lowers position error from 2.00 to 1.24 and release-timing error from 4.3 to 3.3 steps, and raises alignment from 0.22 to 0.53. Orientation barely moves. Units carry places and moments, and the policy supplies the rest of the motion.

Donation follows behaviour, not concept names. In the blend, fields that share the task's object donate 0.22 of the improvement, fields that share its goal 0.18, and fields that share nothing 0.13. The policy's prior knowledge reaches a new task through behavioural similarity more than through the linguistic identity of a concept.

In new scenes, held fields are interchangeable. Served on LIBERO-90, removing a task's largest donors loses success in only 0.25 of the ablations, and the loss agrees weakly with the offline donation (Spearman 0.23). Where the policy never saw the scene, which held field is composed matters little, because none carries the scene's motion.

Why an operator works on one architecture and fails on another. Text-latent interpolation adds a shift to the residual of the encoder that reads the instruction. On pi0 and pi0.5 the shift is about a quarter of that residual (0.28 and 0.23); on X-VLA and SmolVLA it is as large as the residual (1.01 and 1.14) and replaces the instruction's representation, which matches interpolation's failure on SmolVLA (0 of 400). The grasp switch reads the policy's field under the instruction that taught each phase and reproduces that phase's trained motion on every architecture we test. Practice choosing the operator per task is what makes the system architecture-agnostic.

The grade predicts reuse or creation. A new relation or binding in a familiar scene is a recombination of held fields, and reuse serves it on all five policies. A new scene or a new part has no held field that carries its motion, and only a created unit serves it (Section 10).

Composition as transfer. Systematicity, in Fodor's sense, asks that a learner able to perform one arrangement of parts can perform the others. Cognitive psychology documents the same in people as transfer from task to task. A frozen policy shows a measurable version of both: its prior knowledge donates expertise to tasks it was never trained for, often through fields whose names have nothing to do with the new task, and the reach of that donation ends where the parts or the scene are new. There, composition has to create.

12What composition costs

(docs/COST.md, docs/FIT_TIME.md; Figures: h2_pareto_libero90.png, h2_pareto_ood.png.) On pi0.5 and LIBERO-90 the system with task leaves stores 10.2 MB per task against 13.6 MB for per-task adapters and succeeds 994 against 932 of 1,320 times. It pays in fitting time: fitting costs 31.8 A40-minutes per task on average, against 3.5 for a per-task adapter. A unit served as an unmerged LoRA raises the latency of an action chunk from 292 to 437 ms; the grasp switch and interpolation add no measurable latency (291 ms). On Li's suites a held concept costs at most 0.3 MB per task and the grasp switch nothing; the system spends 340 practice episodes per task where Li's methods spend none, and the gated stream of iteration 14 lowers that. Composition buys success and storage, and pays in fitting time and simulator practice.

Fit time (pre-registered F1). 97% of a unit's 39.4-minute fit recomputed the frozen VLM prefix, which never changes because the units live in the action expert. Cached once, the same 1,000-step fit takes 4.6 minutes with equal losses: an 8.6-fold saving with nothing traded. Shorter fits are not free. On held-out flow loss a unit is best near 200 steps, 47% below its 1,000-step value, but the library refit at 200 steps fails non-inferiority on rollouts: 388 against 480 of 580 (difference -0.159, 95% interval -0.255 to -0.072). Held-out likelihood rewards a field that hedges, and success needs units that commit. Rank-4 task leaves pass: the rule of iteration 8 with them succeeds 980 times against 994 of 1,320 with rank-16 leaves (lower bound -0.039), at a quarter of the bytes.

How space and time scale (docs/SCALING.md; Figure: h3_space.png). A per-task method stores one unit per task; the library stores one per concept. On LIBERO-90 the number of distinct concepts grows as N^0.68 over 66 tasks, well above the ideal N^0.5 of a full object-by-goal grid: the tasks cover a small part of that grid, and each needs two or three units. The library without leaves is smaller than a rank-16 adapter per task from the first task, and draws level with rank-4 adapters at about 67 tasks (an extrapolation just past the 66 measured); with rank-16 task leaves it stays above rank-4 adapters, and with rank-4 leaves, which pass non-inferiority above, it draws level with them at about 590 tasks (an extrapolation of the measured storage slopes). On Li's suites a held concept costs nothing and the latents are capped by the training set. In fitting time the library loses: a prototype is fitted once per concept but costs about ten per-task adapters, so the library becomes cheaper than rank-16 adapters only past about 20 thousand tasks under the fitted growth. Storage favours composition as concepts recur. Fitting time favoured per-task adapters, and caching the frozen prefix closes most of that gap: a prototype then fits in 4.6 minutes, close to a per-task adapter's 3.5.

13What does not work

  • Selecting prototypes by a held-out demonstration's likelihood (as Wang et al. do): the right object concept on top for 12 of 29 queries.
  • Placing unit boundaries by the policy's likelihood: no better than random cuts (Section 8).
  • A new object without a demonstration: borrowed prototypes help on 2 of the 17 tasks the policy fails.
  • Re-checking every earlier arrival after each refit (W2): no gain over W1.
  • Prototypes of concepts the policy already holds (Iteration 5's weakness) and noise-space refinement on top of the composition (DSRL on the library).
  • Composing held fields at the prompt in scenes the policy never trained in (Z3, Z5): no gain over the policy alone.

14A data defect

The IPEC port of LIBERO-90 merges tasks that share an instruction: 16 tasks were given another scene's demonstration, including the per-task baselines. Fixed by matching each scene's block of episodes to the task's rendered first frames.

15Limitations

Flow-matching policies only (pi0.5, pi0, SmolVLA, X-VLA, GR00T N1.5; FLOWER on its single suite, LIBERO-Goal, where both pre-registered tests narrowly fail); routed adapter libraries (CLARE) are not rerun here; choosing a held concept's source by practice costs 160 practice episodes per task; LIBERO-90, Li's OOD suites and LIBERO-PRO only; practice needs a simulator; a new object needs its one demonstration; non-inferiority to per-task adapters without task leaves not established at 0.05. The supervised scheme assumes a template: single-step pick-and-place with one object, one goal and one grasp, whose concepts the instruction names. The unsupervised scheme drops the template, but its identification is weak in unseen scenes (0.18 on the approach in LIBERO-90), and its reads there are exploratory pilots or pre-registered tests that fail against the policy alone.

Appendix

AHow this differs from Wang et al. (ICLR 2027)

This work starts from Wang et al.'s program (ICLR 2027 submission, Test-Time Compositional Generalization in Diffusion Models via Prototype Discovery): a pretrained diffusion model already holds reusable prototypes, and an out-of-distribution query can be written as a composition of them. We carry the program from image generation to a frozen robot policy and change four things: what a prototype is (a phase of behaviour, held or fitted), how it is chosen (by practice), how prototypes combine (in time, at the grasp), and how they persist (a continual library). The present system is supervised by the instruction's object-and-goal grammar; Wang et al.'s discovery is unsupervised, and closing that gap is the end goal of this line of work.

BExperimental designs

Policies. Five frozen flow-matching VLAs: pi0.5 and pi0 (openpi, PyTorch), SmolVLA and X-VLA (LeRobot), and GR00T N1.5; each was fine-tuned on LIBERO's 40 training tasks and is never updated. FLOWER is wrapped but trained on one 10-task suite per checkpoint, which does not match the protocol.

Benchmarks. (1) LIBERO-90 tasks the policies never saw, one demonstration per new concept (66 single-step tasks; 29 on the other policies). (2) Li et al.'s compositional OOD suites (20 tasks, each a training task's object paired with another's target; no demonstration of any test task). (3) LIBERO-PRO's task split (a training scene with a new instruction and success test; the 27 of 40 tasks with one object, one goal and one grasp).

Start states. LIBERO's placement sampler under fixed seeds, saved once per task, so every arm is scored from the same never-used states: practice on set a, design decisions on earlier sets, each confirmatory test on a set no decision saw (sets b, c, d, e, f). LIBERO-PRO is also scored on its own published start states.

Protocol. Every confirmatory read is pre-registered (ledger m3_prereg) before its first episode: hypotheses, arms, start states, noise seed. Episodes are paired across arms (same start state, same seed). Tests: exact sign test on discordant pairs; task-level bootstrap 95% interval of the success difference; non-inferiority at a margin of 0.05; Holm correction where hypotheses share a family. 20 test episodes per task.

Metrics. Success on new start states (accuracy); bytes stored per task and cumulatively (space); fit A40-minutes, practice episodes and demonstrations per task, and latency per action chunk (time).

Baselines. Per-task LoRA (rank 16 and 4, with and without the same acceptance test); retrieval (STRAP); noise-space RL on the frozen policy (DSRL); sequential fine-tuning and experience replay through the same stream; Li et al.'s prompt switching and text-latent interpolation with hand-chosen pairs; velocity-field mixing (GPC); activation injection. Every baseline runs on the same frozen policy, tasks, start states and seed.
